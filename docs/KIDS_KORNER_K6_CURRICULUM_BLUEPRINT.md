# Kids Korner K–6 Curriculum Blueprint

**Project:** RootWise / Root$Wise  
**Status:** Architecture source of truth  
**Audience:** Product, curriculum, design, engineering, educator, and AI collaborators  
**Scope:** Penny's closed elementary learning world, kindergarten through grade 6

## Purpose

Kids Korner develops financial decision capacity through a natural K–6 learning progression. It is not a simplified adult financial course. Children revisit the same seven foundational Roots every year while the language, mathematics, consequences, independence, and game mechanics deepen with their development.

Money is the context. The deeper subject is learning to notice, compare, choose, reflect, and understand what a decision changes.

Kids Korner must remain distinct from adult RootWise:

- Penny is the K–6 guide.
- Sage and adult tools remain outside the child space.
- Cash begins in grade 7 with the middle-school Independence Lab.
- Adults and educators retain responsibility for grade placement, privacy, pacing, and real-world action.
- Penny helps children notice and test ideas; she never chooses for them.

## Locked architecture

1. Kids Korner is a closed kindergarten-through-grade-6 experience.
2. The curriculum uses a spiral model: all seven child Roots return every grade at greater depth.
3. Grade determines developmental depth; Root determines the subject.
4. The adult Grove may retain eleven Roots. Kids Korner uses seven foundational child Roots that later unfold into the adult system.
5. The School Fair is the orientation mission before the seven Roots, not a substitute for Root One.
6. The School Fair must have grade-adaptive versions. One unchanged game cannot serve kindergarten through grade 6.
7. Games teach through visible choices and consequences, not through scores, shame, or a hidden “correct” money personality.
8. Progress measures completed thinking and reflection, not agreement with Penny.
9. No ads, purchases, product recommendations, outside child-navigation links, or adult financial products belong in Kids Korner.
10. No free-text personal-information collection belongs in the child experience.

The currently built 10-token School Fair mission is best matched to grades 1–2. Its underlying decision is appropriate across K–6, but the reading level, quantity of choices, mathematical demand, and consequence depth must change by grade.

## Whole-experience flow

1. **Grown-Up Gate** — adult reassurance, safety boundaries, grade placement, accessibility settings, and educator controls.
2. **Penny Welcome** — Penny addresses the child directly and establishes the rule: curiosity matters more than getting everything right.
3. **School Fair Orientation** — the child experiences a choice with a visible limit and learns that Penny will not decide for them.
4. **Kids Grove** — seven Roots appear as connected parts of one living world.
5. **Grade-Level Root Mission** — story, game, consequence, and reflection are matched to the selected grade.
6. **Saved Growth** — the completed Root visibly grows without scores, public ranking, or shame.
7. **Grade Capstone** — the child connects the seven Roots through a final age-appropriate simulation.
8. **Next-Year Bridge** — the world gains new depth; grade 6 ends with a safe transition toward Cash's grade-7 Independence Lab.

## Grade timeline

Every grade experiences all seven Roots. The progression follows children's growing ability to move from concrete recognition to multi-step reasoning, social awareness, and safe independence.

| Grade | Natural learning mode | Financial learning depth | Primary game style | Typical core mission length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Touch, sort, match, repeat | Notice, name, more or less, now or later | Picture sorting, matching, tap-to-choose, read-aloud stories | 3–5 minutes |
| Grade 1 | Count, compare, make one-step choices | Simple limits, tokens, effort, and promises | Drag-and-drop, 5–10 tokens, short branching stories | 5–7 minutes |
| Grade 2 | Sequence steps and predict near consequences | Small plans, goals, earning and spending combinations | Budget baskets, route puzzles, two-round games | 7–10 minutes |
| Grade 3 | Follow systems and connect cause to effect | Receipts, costs, tradeoffs over time, simple records | Store simulations, consequence cards, mystery challenges | 10–12 minutes |
| Grade 4 | Evaluate fairness, patterns, and alternatives | Multi-step planning, borrowing costs, competing priorities | Branching missions, team-resource games, comparison puzzles | 12–15 minutes |
| Grade 5 | Handle complexity and outside influence | Advertising, risk, peer pressure, and multiple goals | Strategy games, ad detective, probability and planning labs | 15–18 minutes |
| Grade 6 | Rehearse independence safely | Longer consequences, systems, leadership, and responsibility | Multi-round simulations, group decisions, system-building games | 15–20 minutes |

## Standard Root mission loop

Every Root uses a predictable rhythm so the cognitive challenge can grow without making navigation unpredictable.

1. **Penny's Story** — a funny, relatable situation introduces the problem.
2. **Notice** — the child identifies what is happening.
3. **Play Lab** — a puzzle or game lets the child test possibilities.
4. **Choice Challenge** — the decision changes what happens next.
5. **Penny Reflection** — button-based questions help the child explain what mattered.
6. **Growth Marker** — the Root visibly grows; there is no grade or shame state.
7. **Replay** — the child can make a different choice and observe a different consequence.

The game never says, “You are bad with money.” It says, “Here is what changed when you chose that.”

## Root One — Me, My Choices & My Money Story

**Financial decision capacity:** Recognize that feelings, experiences, preferences, messages, and influences affect choices.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Name feelings and preferences without calling them good or bad. |
| Grade 1 | Understand that two people can want different things. |
| Grade 2 | Notice where ideas about money and belongings come from. |
| Grade 3 | Identify family habits and repeated messages without judging the family. |
| Grade 4 | Recognize comparison and peer influence. |
| Grade 5 | Separate personal values from advertising and popularity. |
| Grade 6 | Make a decision while noticing pressure, emotion, and identity. |

**Game families:** emotion matching, preference paths, “whose voice is that?” stories, influence detective, and build-your-own decision stories.

## Root Two — Value, Effort & Earning

**Financial decision capacity:** Understand that value can be created through effort, skill, reliability, creativity, and contribution.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Match community work with what it helps provide. |
| Grade 1 | Exchange simple effort for tokens in a pretend world. |
| Grade 2 | Compare time, effort, and skill. |
| Grade 3 | Notice that materials and time use part of what comes in. |
| Grade 4 | See how quality and reliability affect value. |
| Grade 5 | Explore how tools, teamwork, and skill-building change capacity. |
| Grade 6 | Compare safe earning simulations by time, capacity, stability, and dependence. |

**Game families:** community-helper match, repair-the-fair, skill-building quests, production puzzles, and team booth challenges.

## Root Three — Choice, Spending & Tradeoffs

**Financial decision capacity:** Make choices within a limit and recognize what each “yes” makes unavailable.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Choose between two pictured options using five tokens. |
| Grade 1 | Use ten tokens across several fair choices. |
| Grade 2 | Divide a limited amount among fun, food, and keeping some for later. |
| Grade 3 | Compare price, quantity, and what remains. |
| Grade 4 | Manage competing priorities across multiple rounds. |
| Grade 5 | Detect advertising, add-on costs, and pressure. |
| Grade 6 | Plan an event while balancing money, time, people, and future needs. |

**Game families:** School Fair, budget basket, receipt repair, ad detective, route planner, and event planner.

## Root Four — Saving, Waiting & Preparedness

**Financial decision capacity:** Preserve some choices for later and prepare for needs that are not happening yet.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Practice now versus later. |
| Grade 1 | Fill a visible goal jar. |
| Grade 2 | Save across several game rounds. |
| Grade 3 | Handle a surprise without automatically losing the goal. |
| Grade 4 | Balance more than one goal. |
| Grade 5 | Plan for occasional and repeating costs. |
| Grade 6 | Build a buffer and decide when it should or should not be used. |

**Game families:** goal garden, token jar, bridge-to-the-goal, surprise-weather cards, and preparedness camp.

## Root Five — Borrowing, Promises & Trust

**Financial decision capacity:** Understand that borrowing creates a responsibility to return, repay, repair, or rebuild trust.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Borrow and return a shared object. |
| Grade 1 | Connect promises with follow-through. |
| Grade 2 | Track what is borrowed and when it returns. |
| Grade 3 | See how repeated behavior changes trust. |
| Grade 4 | Introduce repayment costs using tokens. |
| Grade 5 | Compare different safe borrowing arrangements. |
| Grade 6 | Examine total cost, late consequences, dependence, and responsibility. |

**Game families:** library return race, promise path, trust tower, repayment puzzle, and equipment-lending simulation.

Kids Korner does not market or recommend credit cards, loans, credit scores, or adult financial products.

## Root Six — Growth, Risk & Ownership

**Financial decision capacity:** Understand that resources can grow, shrink, become unavailable, or require continued care.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Plant something and care for it. |
| Grade 1 | Watch resources change across rounds. |
| Grade 2 | Experience safe uncertainty and chance. |
| Grade 3 | Choose whether to use or reinvest game resources. |
| Grade 4 | Compare owning, borrowing, renting, and sharing. |
| Grade 5 | Explore probability and spreading risk. |
| Grade 6 | Balance growth, loss, time, access, and responsibility. |

**Game families:** seed-to-tree, build-or-use, storm cards, ownership workshop, and risk garden.

## Root Seven — Stewardship, Systems & Legacy

**Financial decision capacity:** Care for resources, participate in shared systems, and understand how today's decisions affect other people and the future.

| Grade | Developmental focus |
| --- | --- |
| Kindergarten | Care for shared toys and spaces. |
| Grade 1 | Decide how to use a shared classroom resource. |
| Grade 2 | Practice generosity with boundaries. |
| Grade 3 | Allocate resources for a small community. |
| Grade 4 | Examine equal versus fair. |
| Grade 5 | Maintain a system instead of merely starting it. |
| Grade 6 | Create rules and resources that remain useful for the next group. |

**Game families:** community garden, shared-supply rescue, town council, fairness lab, build-a-system, and legacy relay.

## School Fair orientation variants

The School Fair introduces the central Kids Korner rule: Penny helps the child notice, but Penny does not choose for them.

| Trail | Grades | Mission design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sprout Trail | Kindergarten | Five tokens, three picture choices, full read-aloud, and one-step reflection. |
| Explorer Trail | Grades 1–2 | Ten tokens, four choices, visible tradeoffs, and button-based reflection. The current implementation belongs here. |
| Builder Trail | Grades 3–4 | Twenty tokens, two rounds, receipts, a surprise event, and changed consequences. |
| Pathfinder Trail | Grades 5–6 | Event planning, time and token limits, peer influence, a future goal, and multiple tradeoffs. |

Every variant teaches the same underlying capacity while matching the learner's reading level, mathematical development, attention span, and ability to understand delayed consequences.

## Game-design rules by grade

### Kindergarten

- Full read-aloud support
- Pictures and objects before written labels
- No more than two or three active choices at once
- Counting and comparison within a concrete visible range
- No timers by default
- Immediate, visible consequences

### Grades 1–2

- Short sentences paired with icons
- One- and two-step choices
- Visible tokens and simple addition or subtraction
- Replay encouraged without penalty
- Reflection through pictures or short buttons

### Grades 3–4

- Multi-step missions with receipts, lists, and simple records
- Consequences that can appear in a later round
- Comparison, categorization, and early strategy
- Cooperative play may appear, but no public ranking

### Grades 5–6

- Longer simulations with interacting limits
- Advertising, peer influence, uncertainty, fairness, and system effects
- Multiple valid strategies with different consequences
- Greater independence while keeping all actions safe and reversible
- Preparation for Cash's grade-7 world without introducing adult financial burdens

## Recognition and progress

Kids Korner recognition celebrates capacity rather than compliance.

- Roots grow visually as missions and reflections are completed.
- Children earn growth markers, not money-personality labels.
- There are no public leaderboards.
- There are no manipulative streaks or loss-pressure mechanics.
- “Wrong” choices become observable consequences and replay opportunities.
- Progress remains private to the child and authorized grown-up or educator.

## Safety and accessibility boundaries

- Penny remains a guide, never a replacement for a parent, guardian, or educator.
- A grown-up or educator sets the grade level and controls the exit.
- No ads, purchases, sales, product recommendations, or outside child-navigation links.
- No collection of names, addresses, financial details, contact information, or open-ended personal disclosures from children.
- No adult credit products or adult financial burdens placed on children.
- Audio, captions, keyboard access, reduced motion, predictable navigation, and sensory controls are part of the experience.
- Color is never the only signal.
- Timed play is optional and off by default.
- Penny never labels a child as good or bad with money.

## Transition to Cash

Grade 6 is the final Penny year. Its capstone should combine identity, value, choice, saving, trust, risk, and stewardship in one multi-round community or event simulation.

Completion does not mean the child has finished financial learning. It means the learner is ready to move from Penny's concrete, playful, adult-bounded sandbox into Cash's grade-7 Independence Lab, where consequences, social pressure, independence, and tradeoffs become more realistic while remaining safe and reversible.

## Build sequence

1. Add the grown-up grade and accessibility settings.
2. Preserve the current School Fair as the grades 1–2 Explorer Trail.
3. Build the kindergarten, grades 3–4, and grades 5–6 School Fair variants.
4. Add the seven-Root Kids Grove architecture and grade-aware mission routing.
5. Build Root One across K–6 before expanding the remaining Roots.
6. Add private grown-up or educator progress visibility.
7. Build grade capstones and the grade-6 transition to Cash.
